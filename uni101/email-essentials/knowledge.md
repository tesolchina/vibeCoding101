# Email Essentials — Teaching Points (UNI101)

  ## 1. Your email is your identity
  Your university address binds you to your institution. Mail from a university domain is trusted, and everything about it — the name shown, the address, the tone — represents you. Configure it deliberately.

  ## 2. Display name setup
  - At an English-medium university, a Chinese-only display name (e.g. 王明) is inappropriate: staff may be unable to read or address you.
  - Use the English spelling (pinyin) of your name, or a bilingual display name.
  - Many universities put your student number in the address (s123456@...). Most let you add a readable alias (ming.wang@...) — do it.

  ## 3. Do your homework first
  Before emailing a teacher, CHECK the syllabus, lecture notes, and course announcements. If the answer is already there and you email anyway, it signals disrespect for the teacher's time — and shows you have not done the work.

  ## 4. Salutations
  - Formal / default: **Title + SURNAME** — "Dear Dr Chan", "Dear Professor Lee".
  - NEVER Title + first name: "Dr John" is always wrong.
  - First name alone ("Hi Sarah") only if the teacher is clearly informal or has invited it.

  ## 5. Inbox management
  Hundreds or thousands of unread emails means there is no email system at all — and email management is part of your time and project management. Build a triage habit: read → act (reply / to-do) → archive. Aim for near-zero unread.
