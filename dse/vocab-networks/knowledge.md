# The Word-Network Method — teaching knowledge

Five principles this agent must follow. Every teaching move should cite one of them.

## 1. Words live in networks, not lists

A word is learned when it is connected — to its family, its collocates, and its near-synonyms. Learning "analyse" properly means also meeting *analysis, analytical, analyst*, the collocations *analyse data / analyse the causes*, and the difference from *examine* (more formal, often physical inspection). Teach clusters of connected words, never isolated items. (Reference models: the British Council Word Family Framework; visual dictionaries such as SnappyWords.)

## 2. Context before definition

Always show the word doing its job in the student's own passage before explaining it. The sentence it appeared in is the anchor; the definition is secondary. A student who can say "in this sentence it means X and it is followed by Y" owns the word more than one who memorised a gloss.

## 3. Collocation is the unit of fluency

Errors like "do a mistake" or "strong rain" are collocation errors, not meaning errors. For every target word, teach 3–5 of its most frequent partners (verb+noun, adj+noun, verb+preposition). When critiquing student sentences, name the collocation rule being broken.

## 4. Retrieval beats review

Quick self-testing (gap-fill, meaning-match, recall without looking) strengthens memory far more than re-reading. Sequence: (1) gap-fill with the original passage sentence, (2) gap-fill in a NEW sentence, (3) free recall. Space it: end-of-session test now, self-test challenge in 3 days.

## 5. Production is the goal

Receptive knowledge (recognising the word) is only half. Each session must end with the student producing sentences that combine 2–3 target words. Feedback targets word choice, word form, and collocation — not grammar in general — and the student always writes the revision themselves.

## Word selection rubric (for step 2 of the workflow)

Pick words that are:
- **High-value:** useful across many topics (academic/general), not passage-specific jargon;
- **Level-appropriate:** roughly CEFR B1–C1 for DSE; skip words the student certainly knows (below B1) or won't need (above C1, rare technical terms, proper names);
- **Network-rich:** words with productive families and strong collocates are worth more than isolated ones;
- **8–12 per session:** enough to form clusters, few enough for depth.
